Overview

A former fragment of the Pakardio–Khalin plate, with mountain ranges, rain-fed forests, and a Mediterranean southern coast.

Geological history

Pakardia is an emergent remnant of the Pakardio–Khalin plate, which rifted away from Kaishel approximately 40 million years ago. The fragment drifted north and entered the latitudinal band of the Known World roughly 15 million years ago.

Relief and climate

Two mountain ranges structure the island-continent's topography. Moisture-bearing winds bring year-round rain to much of the region, supporting temperate rainforest. The southern coast lies in a rain shadow and is influenced by cold offshore currents, producing a Mediterranean-type climate.

Ecological significance

Pakardia's long period of geographic separation and its varied climates provide the setting for distinctive regional communities. Its geological history is important to understanding both its landscapes and its biogeography.
